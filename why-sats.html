Why do requests cost sats?

Short answer: to keep the queue honest and the artists paid. The longer answer, honestly told.

Library tracks — 21 sats

The station already holds a licence for this music: we pay the collecting societies (Buma/Stemra, Sena) that pay rightsholders, and every aired track is logged for exactly that purpose. So your 21 sats are not buying the song — the licence covers that.

The fee is an anti-spam measure: it keeps one joker from flooding the queue, and what it raises helps keep the station's gear running. That's the whole story.

Wavlake tracks — 210 sats

Wavlake artists chose a different deal: value-for-value. No collecting society sits between you and them — listeners pay artists directly, in sats, per play. Wavlake confirmed we may rebroadcast their catalog on exactly that condition.

That's why a Wavlake request costs more: most of it travels on to the artist through their own Lightning split, automatically, on every play. You're not paying a fee — you're paying the musician.

Wavlake's music stays at Wavlake. The station fetches a song only after a listener has paid for it, and keeps that copy so a next request plays without stutter. Before every play the station asks Wavlake again whether the song is still there; if the artist removed it, our copy goes too. Nothing from Wavlake enters the station's own library or rotation, and every single play pays the artist again.

Fair warning, honestly given: V4V is young. Prices here may move as we learn what's fair — and we reserve the right to adjust the request price to keep spam out.

Where to check up on us

Every claim above is meant to survive a fact-check: the honest sats log shows where the numbers come from, the manifesto explains where this is heading, and the roadmap shows the split ladder.

Questions, bug reports, or something that smells off? Join t.me/noderunnersradio and tag @noderunnersfm — the log gets corrected, not defended.

The station is the bar, not the band: request fees keep the lights on and the queue honest — the music itself is paid for through licences or straight to the artist.
