The site now speaks six languages: English, Dutch, German, French, Spanish and Portuguese. Tap a flag at the top and the page changes at once, without a reload. The picture behind the music is yours now. The rotation steps aside for a live DJ. Messages keep their words.

When you watch the stream, you can now choose the picture behind the music. A button in the player opens a picker with 135 pieces of the captain’s own artwork. Pick one and it stays put. You can set a plain background of your own instead, or let it shuffle and fade to another picture about once a minute. Your choice is yours alone, and it stays in your own browser, so nobody else’s screen changes. It is lighter on your data too. The now-playing card still keeps step with what you hear.

  • When a DJ goes live the backdrop picker steps aside, its button hides, and you see the DJ’s own feed just as it is sent. The ship’s own rotation stops for the set, then starts again by itself when the feed ends. That is what it did on 10 September, when DJs took the decks.

  • A message on the broadcast keeps all of its words, always. Only web addresses are checked, against a short list of sites the station trusts. An address that is not on the list is left out, and so is a lookalike that merely carries a trusted name.

Aug 9, 2026

Longer tracks cost a little more — and the DJ mixes step off the request list

Requesting a song is still the flat 21-sat anti-spam donation — about a eurocent, and it stays that way for any normal-length track. What changed: the fee now climbs gently for very long tracks, so one cent can no longer hold the station for an hour. A 21-minute track runs a few hundred sats, an hour around twenty thousand. It is friction that scales with the airtime a request occupies — never a price on the music itself, which the licences we pay already cover.

Two things worth being straight about: a shorter track is never more expensive than a longer one, and this is a first cut — we picked a curve that felt right and we will adjust it on what you tell us.

  • DJ mixes, livesets and sample packs came off the request list. A mix is many songs strung together, not one track — it belongs on the shelf, not in the queue. A genuine hour-long album-as-one-song stays, priced by its length.
  • Wavlake is untouched: still a flat value-for-value tip that goes straight to the artist. Length pricing is only on the ship’s own library.
  • Nothing left the collection — the full index is intact. This only changes what is requestable and what it costs.

The library is open, and artists get paid through Telegram

The station played from a small test folder. It now plays from the music library. 665,944 tracks are on air. That is most of the collection.

The station shuffles the whole library once, then plays through it in order. No track repeats until every track has played. Then it shuffles again.

Search grew with it. The jukebox could find 756 tracks. It can now find over 124,000, and that number is still rising while the library is indexed.

Music that friends gave to the station stays out until each of them agrees. That is their call, not ours.

A new Telegram bot

The jukebox is back on Telegram. Ask for a track, pay the invoice from your own wallet, and it joins the queue.

The bot holds no balance. There is no top-up and no account. Your sats go from your wallet to the station, once, for one song. There is nothing for us to lose on your behalf.

It serves both lanes. The ship’s own library at 21 sats. And the value-for-value lane at 210 sats, where the artist is paid directly, within seconds.

Neither number is a price. 21 sats is about one eurocent. It is there so the queue cannot be flooded.

If you used the old bot, withdraw now

The old jukebox bot held a balance for each user. It runs on a machine we cannot reach. It will be switched off by 1 November 2026, and we cannot recover anything left on it.

Open the old bot and withdraw to your own wallet.

Nobody from Noderunners will ever ask you for a wallet string, a link code, a seed or a password. Anyone who does is stealing from you.

Jul 30, 2026

The sats sail on their own — Wavlake artists get paid automatically

Two days ago the Wavlake lane opened. Today it grew a spine: when you pay 210 sats to hear a Wavlake artist on The Dread Node, the station now forwards the artist’s share automatically, within seconds of your track hitting the queue. No human hands, no waiting, no trust required — a dedicated passthrough wallet receives your sats and sends them straight on through Wavlake, which splits them 90% to the artist. A few sats stay behind as headroom for Lightning’s routing fees — that is the honest cost of the rails, written in the station’s ledger next to every forward.

Every payout lands in a plain-text royalty ledger, and the captain’s console grew a warning light that refuses to stay silent if a forward ever fails. Three artists were paid this way today, on real requests, while we watched: the machine did the work.

The crew side grew too. DJs can now choose if they wish to partake in the v4v flow or not whenever a Host lets them step up to the decks. The jukebox search got lane filters (Wavlake V4V or the ship’s library, with a CC0 lane on the horizon). A hundred and fifty fresh tracks joined the (test) rotation, more inbound, soonish. And any DJ with gear and a place can now flip “I can host at my place” on their own page, plan a session sheet with slots and times, and send it to the captain for approval or rejection. An onboard calendar gets updated on the fly and the crew can stay notified in the Telegram chat.

Tonight the ship goes live again. Come listen.
