Your first sats

A Lightning wallet for the jukebox, set up safely.

To pick songs on the jukebox you pay a few sats over Lightning. For that you need a Lightning wallet: an app on your phone that pays in sats. This page helps you start safely.

We do not make these apps. We earn nothing from them. We cannot fix them for you.

Four rules first

  1. Pocket money only. A phone wallet is like the cash in your pocket. Keep your savings somewhere else.
  2. Install only from the maker's own website. Find the maker's real site first (see below), then use the download link on that site. Fake wallet apps hide in the app stores. They look real, and they steal your sats.
  3. Write your recovery words on paper. Never type them anywhere else. Never take a photo of them. Never put them in the cloud. Whoever has these words has your sats.
  4. Nobody from Noderunners Radio will message you first. We never ask for your words or your sats. Anyone who does is a scammer.

Choose a wallet yourself: five questions

We do not name wallets. Apps change, and one that works today can stop tomorrow. These questions keep working.

  1. Who holds the sats? Self-custodial: you hold the recovery words, and nobody can freeze your sats. Custodial: a company holds them for you, like a bank account. Both can pay the jukebox. Know which one you have.
  2. Can it pay over Lightning? The jukebox shows a Lightning payment code. The wallet must be able to scan it and pay it.
  3. Is its code public? Open-source wallets can be checked by anyone. WalletScrutiny ↗ checks wallets for this and lists each maker's real website.
  4. Is it alive? Look for recent updates and news on the maker's own site. Silence for a long time is a warning.
  5. Does it promise too much? “Earn interest”, other coins, giveaways, a stranger who recommends it to you in a private message: walk away.

Start small

Put in a few sats first. Pay for one song. Only when that works, add more.

Is it the real app? Three checks

  1. The address bar. It must show exactly the maker's address, letter for letter, as WalletScrutiny lists it.
  2. The developer name. In the app store, it must match the name the maker's own site gives.
  3. Not proof: an ad, a top search result, lots of stars or lots of downloads. Fakes have all of those.

Stuck? Ask in the group

Ask in our Telegram group, out in the open. Not everyone there is crew, so never move to private messages.
