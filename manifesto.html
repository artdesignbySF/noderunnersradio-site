2026-07-23 · Noderunners Radio Manifesto

The Public is the DJ

The Noderunners Radio Manifesto — v0.1, July 2026 (12026 HE)
Signed, from a shipwreck, partly in international waters, partly ashore — Noderunners Radio.

If you walk along the coast near Scheveningen, you can sometimes still feel a wisp of freedom in the air. The sea and the beach can do that to a person. Combine it with music, and you have a fantastic concoction — a magical mix everyone would like a taste of.

"What if we could broadcast our music all over the beach? The beach?! LAND! Where laws stop at national borders, radio waves just keep going!"

This is how I imagine the thought process of the pioneers who started Radio Veronica. What a brilliant idea. Sail your little boat just outside the national borders, and start broadcasting — whatever you want to get out there. Whoever wants to listen tunes in; whoever doesn't, ignores it. Perfect.

What else doesn't stop at borders? Information wants to be free, or so it seems. The internet is a good example of this. Bitcoin even more so.

It is a meme-world and we are just living in it

Where Radio Veronica once embodied freedom in its frequencies, flowing over the Netherlands and Belgium, you now only hear the surf — filled with advertising, nonsense, and "the news." Sometimes, if you're lucky, a musical gem suddenly lies in the sand. But often they are like razor clams: expertly and purposefully assembled to do what they were made for — maintaining what was. Because when you pick up such a razor clam on the beach, there is no life left in it.

The same goes for many radio stations, commercial or not. The factory music, pre-cooked and all, is pumped into your ears in bite-sized chunks — if you can still bear to listen to an average radio station for any length of time. This is a little hyperbolic, sure. But it should be clear there is room for improvement.

Enter Noderunners Radio & the Jukebox Bot — the very first Bitcoin-powered radio station, where anyone can be the DJ. No longer do we bow to the dictatorship of the oligopoly that holds sway over the music industry. A new, more melodious tea party has emerged.

What we are building, in plain words

For anyone landing here new — no jargon, no assumptions:

  • A radio station that streams over the internet (video + audio), run as a labor of love for over three years, currently rebuilding itself from a shipwreck into a proper vessel.
  • A jukebox: any listener can pay a few sats (the smallest pieces of a bitcoin) to request a song. The payment is instant, borderless, and goes where it should. Currently, this payment is purely for anti-spam protection and sits at 21 sats per song. This will change and should perhaps reflect the duration of a song too: longer song = more sats to pay.
  • Live sessions: guest DJs come aboard, stream their set through our channel, and receive their fair share of everything the audience donates while they play — automatically, without paperwork, without waiting, without a middleman skimming.
  • A captain's console: one login for the station keeper to approve DJs, run the money switch, and keep the ship honest.
  • Everything transparent: what comes in, how it splits, and where it goes is communicated honestly and in good faith. FOSS (Free as in Freedom, Open Source Software) provides the backbone.
How the money flows — the honest version

During live sessions (today, the rebuild era): The station keeps 75%, the guest DJ pool receives 25%. Why so station-heavy? Because the studio is half-broken and being rebuilt with these very sats. This is a rebuild rate, stated openly, and it is designed to fall: as the ship heals, the split steps down — 75/25 → 69/31 → 60/40 — in the DJs' favor.

The end state (when we go pro): When the house is in order and Noderunners Radio matures into a true FM/AM broadcaster — one that quietly transmits the state of the Bitcoin network alongside the music — the ladder flips to Pareto:

  • Live sessions: 21% radio / 79% to the DJs.
  • Jukebox requests: a song from the ship's library costs 21 sats. That fee is purely anti-spam and stays with the station; the music itself is paid for through the licences. A Wavlake song costs 210 sats and 100% of that travels directly to Wavlake, and as we understand it, they give 90% to the artist, keep 5%, and kick back 5% to the station (this last part we can verify) — value-for-value.
  • Background playlist, nothing requested: the classical licensing organizations get paid, honestly, like the law asks — until something better replaces them.

The pool principle: When multiple DJs play one live session, the DJ share is one pool, divided evenly among everyone on the lineup that night. No per-song scorekeeping, no timing games, no leaderboard poison. Want to tip your favorite DJ extra? Do it directly, to their own address — outside the pool. Both things stay pure.

The dust principle: We work in whole satoshis only. Donations pile up in a pot during the session; at settlement each DJ receives their share rounded down to whole sats, paid in a single fee-free payment (besides network routing fees, that is (those are not in our control)). Every leftover fraction — anything too small to split honestly — trickles into the radio's general donation jar, to keep the keeper alive and build this project further for the benefit of all. Dust is never lost, never harms anyone, and never silently disappears into fees.

An honest footnote on fees: when a payout has to travel across the wider Lightning network — for example, to a wallet outside our station — the network itself takes a routing fee along the way. That part is physics, not us. Payouts to station wallets travel internally and pay no fee at all, and settlement pays each DJ once per session, so fees can never quietly eat the small stuff.

The identity principle: One DJ = one name = one Lightning address, set in stone at registration. No duplicates, no account bloat, no confusion about who gets paid.

The vision — a Cambrian explosion in FOSS

The Jukebox Bot is Free (as in freedom) Open Source Software. Anyone can pick it up and build with it. The pieces are being modularized so that, in time, software modules can hook in and out of each other on the fly to provide a service.

We envision a future where musicians hash their work to the timechain as proof of creation. A musician generates a unique digital fingerprint of their song and embeds it in a Bitcoin transaction — an immutable, verifiable timestamp that the work existed, made by its maker. This can grow into an organic replacement for legacy copyright: when a listener requests a song, sats flow directly and instantly to the musician's Lightning address, bypassing the royalty-collection bureaucracy entirely. And someday, when new work samples older work, value can trickle down the tree to everyone who contributed — messy at first, maybe, but it might blossom into something grand.

Top-down systems, where a central power wields the scepter, are slowly but steadily being overgrown by the ivy of technological progress. A redistribution of power, back to the individual.

Any amateur can build a radio receiver with some copper wire and a little tinkering. We don't have an antenna yet — for now we're bound to the waters of the internet — but our ambitions are high. The passion and the puzzle pieces are there, and they will one day be put together. Why not by you and me?

Welcome to the most confusing decade ever

If you're trying to find the signal through the noise, you're not alone. This transition will not be smooth everywhere; evolution is always argued and fought from different perspectives. Behind the scenes, people are working to make the shift as painless as possible for as many as possible. Noderunners Radio and the Jukebox Bot contribute in their own way: right now, that means building the machinery that rewards musicians and DJs directly, the moment their work brings someone joy.

If this is all incomprehensible language to you — come play with what already works. That's the whole point.

Current status

This project is in its early days, even after ~three years on the air. The people building it have done so at a loss — when time and life permitted, as a hobby, out of love for music and community. If that resonates, support us however and whenever you can.

Thank you for your time and attention. Please, play us a song.

Noderunners Radio
